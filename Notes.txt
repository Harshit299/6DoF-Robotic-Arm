TASK SPACE:
1. solve IK at every time step
2. Path of EE is our concern
3. If we solve IK only at the waypoints its path would be arbitrary (not in our control)

JOINT SPACE:
1. Solve IK only at waypoints
2. Path of EE is NOT our concern
3. Joint angles are interpolated between the two waypoints using linear, cubic, quintic etc. trajectories
4. Both jerky and smooth motion of joints are possible (depends on type of interpolation), but motion of end effector may     still be jerky

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LINEAR INTERPOLATION: Joints and EE starts and stop suddenly with jerk and velocity remains constant

CUBIC INTERPOLATION: Velocity of joints and EE is zero at start and end points but they start with sudden jerk and stop with sudden jerk i.e acceleration at the start and end points is mathematically infinite

QUINTIC INTERPOLATION: Velocity and acceleration both are smooth i.e joints and EE start and stop very smoothly

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

TYPES OF ROTATIONS OF A COORDINATE FRAME:
1. EXTRINSIC Rotation: Every rotation occurs around the axes of the fixed global coordinate system. The axes never move.
2. INTRINSIC Rotation: Every rotation occurs around the axes of the local coordinate system, which moves and rotates with the object after every step.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

JACOBIAN:
1. doesn't depend on where the centre of coord frame and actual joint centre are located
2. jacobian only requires:
   axis of rotation or translation
   origin of coord frame must be lying on that axis

3. gives the instantaneous velocity at particular instant of time wrt base frame

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SVD is Ultimate:
1. it takes a matrix of any order (not necessary square)
2. breaks it down into UEVT
3. E is diagonal matrix consisting of singular values along diagonal
4. singular values are basically lengths of semi-axes of manipulability ellipsoid
5. If we calculate the Singular Values via SVD, we don't just get the warning sign of failing direction. we get the exact mathematical way required to isolate that failing direction, apply a damping factor (lambda) to that specific sigma, and safely push the arm through the singularity without crashing the code!
6. If you feed an m x n matrix into an SVD algorithm, U will be an m x m square matrix.
7. U represents the physical output space. Because it has 6 columns, it provides exactly 6 orthogonal 3D vectors representing the principal Cartesian directions our end-effector can move (3 translations, 3 rotations).
8. The columns of the V matrix are called the Right Singular Vectors. They represent the exact combinations of joint velocities required to move along the axes of your manipulability ellipsoid.
9. If U is the "output space", then V is the "input space" (our motors).

CONDITION NUMBER:
1. ratio of max singular value to min singular value in E
2. it only tells that some of the directions in manipulability ellipsoid are easily reachable while other are not, but doesn't tell which of those directions are?
3. It tells use how close we are to the singularity
4. high condition no. --> close to singularity

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DETERMINANT:
1. It tells us is the robot currently IN a singularity? (Yes or No)
2. As your robot moves toward a singularity, the determinant might stay seemingly healthy (e.g., $0.9$, $0.8$, $0.7$) and    then suddenly drop off a cliff to $0.0001$ in a single millimeter of movement. It does not provide a reliable "distance" to the singularity
3. Det = sigma1 x sigma2 x ... x sigma6
